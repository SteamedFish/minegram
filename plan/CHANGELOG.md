# Changelog

All notable Minegram changes are documented here. The format follows Keep a Changelog and the project uses semantic versioning once the first playable release is tagged.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- Star Battle **fallback boards are now told to the player**. The engine reports `fallback: true` when the budget-expiry fallback (the retired strips-and-sea construction) printed the board — a legal, unique, connected board that looks completely different from the spanning-tree stream — but the flag died at the Worker seam and the player had no way to know why the board looked unlike anything else the generator makes. The flag now travels worker → store → surface: validated on the wire like every other untrusted field (optional; absent reads as not-fallback, a present non-boolean is rejected), carried on `StarBattleSnapshot`, and said once as a quiet caption inside the board card — "printed by the reserve layout, generation used its whole time budget; the board is fully valid with exactly one solution" / 「这盘棋由备用布局生成——生成时间用满了预算。它完全合法，答案仍然唯一。」 Not an error, not a warning, not a grade. `waves` deliberately stays off the snapshot: it is a retired solver-grind instrument with no player-facing meaning, and the snapshot is a projection of what the surface needs.
- Star Battle **challenging's tier description no longer promises a shape the generator does not gate**. The clause "no single colour owns a whole row or column" described the whole-line exclusion the old challenging tier enforced — a gate deleted with the walk generator. The shipped predicate is `grade.k === 1`, which excludes nothing about shape, so the clause was false as a guarantee (whole-line colours are now merely rare). Both locales now state only what the grading enforces — the basic rules stall, one idea beyond them finishes the board — and a test scans the description block for shape-guarantee vocabulary so a structural promise cannot quietly reappear in that copy.

### Changed
- Star Battle **generator replaced**: the strips-and-sea painting is retired as the primary construction and survives only as the budget fallback. The shipped construction lays a valid star arrangement, cuts n − 1 random edges off a randomised spanning tree of the four-neighbour graph, **rejects unless every region holds exactly one star**, then applies **guided counterexample repair** — recolouring a cell that is a star in a counterexample but blank in the intended answer, which breaks that counterexample while leaving the intended answer's per-colour counts untouched. Repair is not optional: among mine-balanced connected colourings, uniqueness-among-balanced is 85% at n = 4 and **0% from n = 6 up**, and running the full catalogue solver with case-splitting on every surviving candidate found boards that were not unique at all. **Guided repair is mandatory** — restricting moves to cells in (alternative − answer) converted 55% / 31% / 14% at n = 6 / 8 / 10 with dead ends showing zero legal moves (spanning-tree regions are corridor-like and nearly every cell is an articulation point), while enumerating every structure-legal recolour and scoring by the exact counter at **cap 16** converted 100% / 100% / 69%. Cap 2 is gradient-blind.
- Star Battle **acceptance gate is now exact uniqueness**, `countStarSolutions(colours, n, 2) === 1`, with a budget-exhausted count treated as a rejection and never as an acceptance. The propagation certificate (solvable with no guessing) is *strictly stronger* and was the gate for most of this work; measured, it accepts **zero boards at n = 8, 10 and 12** and was costing the product. It is retained as a difficulty signal and a playability diagnostic, never as admission. The contract requires a unique solution and a sufficient condition had been quietly promoted over the requirement.
- Star Battle **`starter` and `steady` are removed**; three tiers ship: `challenging` (k = 1), `expert` (k = 2), `contradiction` (k = −1). Removed rather than retuned because the k = 0 class is unreachable under the new construction (8% / 10% / 0% at n = 6 / 8 / 10) — a shallow board has many valid answers, so a unique shallow board needs deep repair, and deep repair cannot produce a shallow board. **The game therefore has no genuinely easy mode**; the fastest default (n = 8 `challenging`, 162 ms) still starts at roughly one assumption deep, since only ~10% of boards at that size are solvable by the four base rules. Bands were derived from a measured distribution and an unreachable band is recorded as a fact rather than widened.
- Star Battle **board sizes are 4..10 with a default of 8** (was 4..15 default 10), read from `MIN_STAR_SIDE` / `MAX_STAR_SIDE` / `DEFAULT_STAR_SIDE` as the single source of truth. Measured per-tier cost, 30 generations per cell: n = 6 p50 8 / 12 / 39 ms, n = 8 p50 162 / 446 / 449 ms, n = 10 p50 9.1 / 6.0 / 20.3 s with 1/30, 0/30 and 2/30 falling back — so n = 10 is marked as the slow option on the control itself and n ≤ 8 is far inside its 15 s budget.
- Star Battle **colour shapes are no longer bands around one dominant colour**: measured largest colour region 22–51% at n = 6–10, against 91–94% for the retired construction. The hub the player objected to came from the construction, not the rules — the four boards they singled out as interesting have none (largest region 25–40%).
- Test suite timeout raised from vitest's 5 s default to 30 s in `vite.config.ts`: `walkStarBattleBoard`'s own 5 s budget was the same number, so a test exercising a walk could never distinguish "walk spent its budget" from "test hung". CI had been failing on `master` for that reason.

### Added
- Star Battle **technique tiers re-tiered on witness identity** (`src/engine/starBattle/construct.ts`, `feasibility.ts`): after three measured mechanisms (seed-tier rotation `fcab9d1`, MCMC rejection drift `f735a0f`, both together) all landed challenging's (k, witness) class-modal share on 1.000 (`{c1}`), the conclusion drawn and acted on is that the concentration is a property of the puzzle-class space "base stalls AND one idea finishes it", not of any generator — so the CONTRACT changed instead of the sampler. `challenging` is now k = 1 whose minimum-basis witness is NOT line confinement (`techniqueTierAcceptsBasis`: `rules ∩ {c1, c2} = ∅`, exact under the canonical witness choice — had any single line-confinement subset solved, canonical order would have reported it). `expert` (k = 2) and `contradiction` (k = −1) are unchanged. The `StarTechniqueTierBudgetExhaustedError` now carries `lastWitness` for diagnostics. **Measured honestly (2026-10-07, deep probes, this machine): the non-freebie k = 1 class decays with side** — n = 5 ≈ 18.8% per walk (Wilson 12.2–27.7%, G ≈ 1), n = 8 ≈ 4.2% (1.6–10.2%, G worst 0.55), n = 10 ≈ 0.8% (0.2–3.0%, G ≈ 0.33 — generations land about a third of the time, measured 8/12 seeds), n = 15 0/72. Where the class exists the witnesses are genuinely {c3}/{c4} (generation-level: n = 4 all {c4}, n = 5 {c3}×3/{c4}×3, n = 8 {c3}×7/{c4}×1) — challenging's `{c1}` share fell from 1.000 to 0.000 by construction. The re-measured availability matrix (feasibility.ts module doc): challenging `available` at n = 4, 5, `unreliable` at n = 7, 8, `unavailable` at n ≥ 9 — including the default 10 — where the picker's honest signal now reads `unavailable`. An early 156-walk probe read 0 hits at n = 10; a deeper 240-walk probe found 2 — the class is rare there, not empty, and the docs record both measurements. Expert/contradiction availability moved only through the shared walk stream.
- Star Battle **shaping give-up is no longer a generation failure** (`construct.ts`, per the player's ruling 「偶尔小概率出现而不是一直出现，没关系，只要合法，不用刻意排除」): the steady hub-free shaping descent still runs and still delivers structural variety (hubs 6/6 → 0/6), but on budget exhaustion `resolveShapedSteadyColours` now catches the typed `StarShapeBudgetExhaustedError`, ships the painted (legal, unshaped) board, and records `StarGeneratedBoard.shapeAudit.gateMet: false` — an audit field, not a rejection. Construction-tier generation therefore has no player-facing failure path at all (the feasibility probe's construction-tier catch was removed with it). Measured 0/3000 natural give-ups at n = 6..10; the fallback is pinned through the wrapper's budget overrides, which exist only because natural seeds never give up.

### Added
- Star Battle `techniqueTierAcceptsBasis(difficulty, basis)` (`construct.ts`): the single full tier contract (k target plus challenging's witness-identity rule), consumed by both the acceptance gate (d) and the feasibility probe so the measured availability always describes the shipped generator. `TECHNIQUE_TIER_TARGET` remains the k component.
- Star Battle `resolveShapedSteadyColours` (`construct.ts`): the shaping give-up wrapper (fallback + `gateMet` audit), exported for the forced-give-up tests; its budget overrides default to the shipped `SHAPE_MAX_ATTEMPTS` / `SHAPE_WALL_CLOCK_MS`.
- Star Battle **rejection (MCMC) drift over legal colourings** (`src/engine/starBattle/drift.ts` + `drift.test.ts`): the witness-axis attack on the technique tiers' (k, witness) monotony, built and measured BEFORE any change to the shape gate per the lane's sequencing safety rule. The drift repaints a cell to a 4-neighbour's colour (Metropolis–Hastings-corrected — the correction line is kept and its provable no-op property under this kernel is pinned by a direct test), hard-rejects region disconnections, and accepts iff the board has exactly one star solution. The uniqueness gate enforces the acceptance-test boundary structurally: the full depth-0 catalogue MAY shortcut an acceptance (a complete sound-rule solve is a free certificate for the k ≥ 0 majority) and is NEVER a rejection gate — a board it cannot solve falls through to the budgeted exact counter, which rejects non-uniqueness AND budget exhaustion (fail closed), counting exhaustions as the audited residual-bias channel. Budgets are proposal counts (deterministic); wall clock is a give-up-and-restart guard whose exhaustion throws the typed error, never returns a short-drift board. **Measured honestly: the headline question is a NULL** — at n = 10, drifted boards under the `challenging` gates (base places zero AND minimum basis k = 1) come out 10/11 class {c1}, statistically identical to the walk's 11/12, and the null is not a mixing-time artifact (checkpoint legs leave any start basin within 2000 proposals and converge to the same base-placing profile). Two independent samplers agreeing on the same conditional distribution is the measured evidence that the {c1} concentration is a property of the puzzle-class space, not a generator artifact. The economics fail too: ~24–31 ms/proposal at n = 15 (exact-counter rejections dominate), ~22 walk+drift cycles per accepted n = 10 board, zero challenging hits across 12 n = 15 drift legs at budget 800. Consequence: the drift is NOT wired into technique-tier generation and `STAR_TIER_SHAPE_GATE` stays — a gate left in with an honest explanation beats a gate removed into a 30-seconds-per-board regression shipping the same {c1} puzzle. The module ships tested because the drift IS the measured-right instrument for the structural axis (sea starts lose the hub and the line owner within hundreds of accepted steps) and for any future tier whose contract drops "base places zero" (drifted k = 1 boards already show witnesses {c1}/{c3} with base placing 0–7 stars). The full record lives in the drift.ts module doc.
- Star Battle **board signatures and generator input rotation** (`src/engine/starBattle/signature.ts`, `construct.ts`), answering the player's 「每一关的思路都一样，玩久了很无趣」 with the measured diagnosis that the technique tiers were monotone at GENERATION, not difficulty: under one fixed walk input the descent is an attractor ladder (alternative meters `[c3]`, `[c4]`, `[c3,c4]` reproduce meter `[]`'s trajectory attempt-for-attempt), so every steady-seeded walk ends at witness `{c1}` and no acceptance predicate can fix that — rejecting the modal signature rejects 100% of reachable endpoints. The shipped fix injects diversity at the walk's INPUTS: `TECHNIQUE_WALK_INPUT_MENU` + a per-request-seed phase rotate which construction tier seeds each generation's descent (`techniqueTierWalkInput`), while acceptance stays byte-identical. The signature `(k, witness, witnessWaves, firstStarWave, baseFreebies, coreScore)` is measured on the accepted endpoint AFTER every gate (`StarGeneratedBoard.signature`), never fed back into the descent — a `?raw` source guard in `signature.test.ts` fails the build if `walk.ts` ever imports the signature module — because a signature participating in the search re-creates the non-monotone descent that killed five earlier constructions. Supporting measurements: `catalogue.ts` reports `firstStarWave` (tentative case-split placements excluded via save/restore, pinned by a no-leak test); `structure.ts` gains `measureStarStructuralCore` (the five-predicate sea profile, scored as the per-colour max); the hand-derived fixtures moved to `fixtures/handBoards.ts` so all lanes pin the same arrays. **Measured honestly (2026-10-07, n = 10 and n = 15, 8-board windows): the (k, witness) class modal share the complaint is about does NOT reach the ≤ 0.5 target** — challenging stays 1.0 (`{c1}`) at both sizes even with rotation engaged; expert moves 0.75 → 0.625 (n = 10) and 0.875 → 0.75 (n = 15); starter-seeded walks skew k = 2 (roughly doubling expert per-walk acceptance) and break the sea profile (coreScore 4 replaces 5 on a third to a half of rotated boards, line-owner disappears) but the `{c3}` reference class was not observed in 30 starter-seeded walks on this build. The full-tuple modal share improves (challenging n = 15: 0.5 → 0.125) and the rotation's determinism per (n, seed, tier) is pinned. Costs: generation wall-clock roughly doubles at n = 10 and ~9× at n = 15 (measured medians 5.7 s challenging / 5.1 s expert at n = 15, zero give-ups over the pin seeds), which the n = 15 test ceilings now document. The `contradiction` tier's menu is a deliberate singleton: a k = −1 board's witness is empty by definition, so class diversity is structurally impossible there, and rotating starter in was measured to only burn budget (126 s vs 80 s per 8 boards with zero class gain) — per the study, its cost profile is left exactly as shipped, and n = 15 contradiction remains unshippable within budget (0/12 generation attempts both before and after; the picker already reads `unavailable` there). `modalSignatureShare` is generator-side measurement only and is not surfaced to the player.
- Star Battle **catalogue result `firstStarWave`**: the 1-based wave index of the first star the run's closure placed (0 when none). Case-split-committed stars sit between waves and are reported through `csPasses`; tentative trial placements are rolled back with the rest of the tentative run's instrumentation.
- Star Battle **structural-core profile** (`measureStarStructuralCore`, `structure.ts`): the five measured sea predicates (owns a whole line, is the largest region, spans ≥ n − 1 columns, covers ≥ 35%, touches ≥ 3 borders), scored as the MAX over colours so a board whose predicates are each held by some colour but never all by one (the k = 2 hand fixture scores 4 that way) is separated from the full monoculture.

### Removed
- Star Battle **the MCMC rejection drift** (`src/engine/starBattle/drift.ts` + `drift.test.ts`, shipped measured in `f735a0f` and called by nothing): the re-tier on witness identity was the third and last mechanism aimed at the witness axis; the drift's own measured record — drifted boards under the challenging gates came out 10/11 class {c1} at n = 10, checkpoint legs from {c1}/{c3}/sea starts all leave their basin within one 2000-proposal leg, n = 15 economics fail (zero challenging hits across 12 legs, 5–11 s per accepted n = 10 board) — says it is the right instrument for the structural axis and the wrong one for the witness axis, and the new contract does not need it. This repo does not keep unreachable code as a safety net: the module and its tests are deleted, and the load-bearing record stands in `signature.ts`'s module doc (with the structural-axis findings: sea starts lose the hub and the line owner within hundreds of accepted steps; where the remaining class diversity lives — witnesses {c1} AND {c3} with base placing 1–7 stars, a different contract than "base places zero").
- Star Battle `isStarTierFeasible(n, tier): boolean` (`feasibility.ts`) and its tests: the export had no production caller — `src/App.tsx` may not import engine values (`src/ui/layerBoundary.test.ts`) and the surface's wrapping prop was deleted in `672413b`. The now-false doc comment claiming "the boolean the surface wraps" is deleted with it; `measureStarBattleTierFeasibility`, `readStarBattleTierFeasibility` and `clearStarBattleTierFeasibilityCache` remain the only UI surface.
- Star Battle (星战), a second game shipped as a peer of Minegram behind a new game picker that is the app's front door on every load (`src/ui/components/GamePicker.tsx`): one card per game with name and a one-line blurb, in both locales. `GamePicker` supports a per-game record and a last-played ring, but `src/App.tsx` passes neither yet — no game tallies those figures — so every card reads "Not played yet"; the empty state is deliberate and tracked in `plan/TODO.md`. The picker is presentational — props in, callbacks out — and the committed surface (`src/ui/components/StarBattleSurface.tsx`) plus store (`src/ui/starBattleStore.ts`) mirror Minegram's shape.
- Star Battle rules as implemented: an n × n grid (default 10, supported 4..15 per `MIN_STAR_SIDE` / `MAX_STAR_SIDE` / `DEFAULT_STAR_SIDE` in `src/domain/starBattle.ts`) with exactly n stars and n colours covering every cell; exactly one star per row, column and colour; and no two stars within Chebyshev distance 1. Four is the floor because the stars form a column permutation with `|T(r) − T(r+1)| >= 2` and no such permutation exists at n = 2 or 3; 15 is a product choice — the largest board the shipped palette and grid already cover. Play contract (`src/application/starBattleReducer.ts`): every one of the n² cells must carry a correct mark, correct marks lock and silently refuse later assertions, a wrong **star** costs one life without refund, lives default to 5 and clamp at zero, zero loses, re-assertion is free and silent, and a `'clear'` retraction returns a cell to unmarked for free so a misclick cannot void a round. A correct star also **auto-fills** its rest-of-row, rest-of-column, other cells of its colour and 3 × 3 neighbourhood as locked blanks, in the same commit. Pointer: a tap or left-click marks a star, right-click or a 500 ms long-press toggles blank, drags paint with set semantics (each cell at most once per stroke). Keyboard: one roving tab stop, arrows / Home / End, Space or Enter for blank, `s` or `*` for a star, Backspace / Delete to retract. Generation runs in a typed Worker with cancellation and identity-based stale-result protection; failures surface with a retry. A won round holds its banner 2500 ms and then generates the next board automatically, the interlude never elapsing while the document is hidden, and the banner's own button starts the next board immediately. Every cell's full state is in its `aria-label`, with one polite `role="status"` region for wrong marks, proximity conflicts and the round's end; palette and grid are pure CSS so larger boards scroll inside their pane (`src/styles/starbattle.css`).
- Star Battle uniqueness **by construction, certified on every board** (`src/engine/starBattle/`). A board is solvable exactly when an admissible column permutation selects n pairwise-distinct colours; the CHAIN generator (`construct.ts`) paints each row's star cell with its proof-order index and flows decoys forward, so every proof region has exactly one viable cell and the colour rule forces it — all n stars forced, therefore unique. Acceptance is the wave propagation solver (`propagate.ts`: freeze, deduce every forced move, apply simultaneously, count one wave), which solves to completion — strictly stronger than a solution count of 1 — so every generated board carries its own uniqueness certificate and no counting runs in production. `countStarSolutions` (`count.ts`) remains an independent exact counter for test cross-checks (exhaustive agreement at n ≤ 10) with a budget-limited variant whose exhaustion can never be misread as a count. Rejection sampling was measured and rejected: uniqueness is measure-zero among well-spread colourings past about n = 8. The generator is described in full under the strips-and-sea entry below.
- Star Battle difficulty, **five tiers named by how many independent techniques a solver must use**, measured and enforced per accepted board rather than asserted. `starter` / `steady` are finished by the basic rules alone (shallow ≈ 3 waves, long chain 13–29 waves by size); `steady` is explicitly **not** a technique tier — it is k = 0 and long, i.e. routine (`steady` inherits the old `challenging` bands byte-identically). `challenging` (挑战) stalls the basic rules and needs exactly one idea whose witness is **not** the whole-line freebie c1/c2 (re-tiered on witness identity in the current release — see the `### Changed` entry above); `expert` (专家) needs two; `contradiction` (反证) stalls the basic rules **and every confinement subset**, so only proof by contradiction works — measured at 60/60 acceptance for n = 8/9/10 with every board at a single assumption level. Because the non-freebie k = 1 class decays with size (per-walk acceptance n = 4 structurally all, n = 5 18.8%, n = 8 4.2%, n = 10 0.8%, n = 15 0/72), `challenging` is a small-side tier: solid at n = 4–5, marginal at n = 7–8, and `unavailable` at n = 6 and n ≥ 9 — including the default 10 — where the picker reports it honestly through the availability machinery. The UI labels them 入门 / 进阶 / 挑战 / 专家 / 反证, using 反证 rather than 矛盾 because 矛盾 already denotes the adjacency-conflict outline. Technique tiers are built by a seeded descent search (`walk.ts`) that recolours single cells until the basic rules stop progressing, and each accepted board is re-verified by enumerating all sixteen technique subsets. **This is not a calibrated human-difficulty scale and is not presented as one:** against four boards the player singled out as interesting the count does not fit — one of them needs a single technique but takes 12 propagation waves to pay off, where our easiest single-technique boards take 4 — and wave count does not separate the levels either, re-measured on the current matching engine (single-technique 4–10 waves at both n = 10 and n = 15, medians 5–8; two-technique 4–14; contradiction stall-runs 1–4). The preference persists under `minegram.star-battle.difficulty` and is never a generation setting. Generation costs ~ 1.1 ms at n = 10 and ~ 2.8 ms at n = 15 for the basic tiers; the technique tiers search, at ~ 36 ms and ~ 150 ms at n = 10.
- Star Battle's **technique solver was non-monotonic and made boards unsolvable by giving the solver more rules** (`catalogue.ts`). Zones were derived from a union closure over colour footprint masks capped at `popcount <= 3`, so extra rules' sound blanks could shrink a footprint until a legal zone became unrepresentable: measured, `{base,c1,c2}` solved a board 10/10 while adding `c3` **or** `c4` stalled it at 0/10. It is replaced by an uncapped Hall/matching propagator (starless colours ↔ starless lines, Kuhn matching with Dulmage–Mendelsohn SCC analysis), monotone by construction because sound blanking only removes graph edges, and it removes an artificial `j <= 3` limit that had misfiled 21 boards as "requires contradiction" when they only needed `j >= 4` confinement. Because upward-closure is no longer free, `measureMinimumBasis` now enumerates all sixteen subsets on **every accepted board** and asserts it, so acceptance can never infer a subset solve from a full-catalogue solve.
- `measureMinimumBasis` counts **ideas**, not rule ids: `c1` and `c2` are one generator under two labels (`catalogue.ts` dispatches `j === 1 ? 'c1' : 'c2'`), so counting ids double-counted one technique. The leave-one-out fingerprint is retired as a grading signal entirely — it reports a rule as load-bearing even when another single rule reaches the same place, which is how a board could read as "requires c2" while all four of `c1`..`c4` solved it independently.
- The catalogue's independent oracle ground truth is now **committed** (`src/engine/starBattle/fixtures/oracle2/*.jsonl`, ~131 KiB) instead of read from gitignored scratch, and the suite's skip path is deleted: a missing fixture now fails the suite instead of silently disabling the whole validation.
- A Star Battle **board-size selector** (`setSide(n)` in `src/ui/starBattleStore.ts`, a `SegmentedControl` in `src/ui/components/StarBattleSurface.tsx`), so the supported range 4..15 is reachable instead of the store always sending the default 10. An out-of-range n is refused silently without throwing, a change is a no-op while a board is generating, and the choice persists under `minegram.star-battle.side` while keeping the current seed and tier. `src/App.tsx` reads the bounds from `MIN_STAR_SIDE` / `MAX_STAR_SIDE` rather than repeating literals, which is why those three names joined the sanctioned-constant allowlist in `src/ui/layerBoundary.test.ts` alongside `MIN_BOARD_SIDE` / `MAX_BOARD_SIDE`. The checked option is the live board's own `puzzle.n`, so the control always reflects what is on screen.
- Star Battle **lives replace the score**, and a correct star **auto-fills its exclusions**. Both came out of a phone play-test. The old `score` only ever fell by one per mistake, so it was lives under a misleading name; it is now `lives` (`MIN_STAR_LIVES` 1, `MAX_STAR_LIVES` 9, `DEFAULT_STAR_LIVES` 5 — the player's 「3 只是我随便想的数字」 left to judgement, and 5 because the hard tier is ~19 deduction waves while a wrong star is the only way to lose a life). `setMaxLives(n)` in `src/ui/starBattleStore.ts` mirrors `setSide` exactly — silent refusal out of range, no-op while generating, same seed and tier on relaunch — and persists under `minegram.star-battle.lives`; the three constants joined the sanctioned-constant allowlist for the same reason the side bounds did. A wrong **blank** is deliberately not chargeable, because an untrue blank is indistinguishable from a player's own note.
- `fillStarExclusions` in `src/application/starBattleReducer.ts` fills a **correct** star's rest-of-row, rest-of-column, other cells of its colour and 3 × 3 neighbourhood as `STAR_LOCKED` blanks in the same commit. It fires only on a correct star, so it records deduction the rules already imply and never discloses where a remaining star is; it structurally cannot overwrite a mark the player made, so a wrong star already paid for survives; and it is idempotent, because every write lands on a cell that is blank in the solution. It is free, and the win check runs after it so the batch placing the last star cannot soft-lock a round. Verified independently over 24 boards × 3 tiers: after placing one correct star every other solution star is still unmarked, which is the failure mode that would silently make a round unwinnable.
- Star Battle **gestures made discoverable on touch**, and the redundant counters deleted. A play-test on a phone reported 「不知道怎么切换雷或者空白」 — touch has no right-click and a 500 ms long-press is invisible, so the shipped left-click-cycles-blank model left marking a mine undiscoverable. A tap now marks a **star** (the assertion the game is about) and right-click or long-press toggles **blank**; drag still paints with the stroke's own tool, and the rules block above the board states all of it in words. The row / column / colour counter groups and their ✓/✗ badges are removed at the player's request (「我感觉上面的图例是冗余的…不如改成游戏规则说明+图例」) — they only restated the board — and replaced by that rules block plus a mark legend. The proximity-conflict outline is kept, since adjacency is the one rule with no count to read off the board. Locked cells now render from the solution rather than from the mark code, because auto-fill writes locked blanks the previous renderer counted as stars.
- A shared in-app **game bar** names the current game on both screens and returns to the picker, with exactly one such control per playing screen (a test asserts the surface's own back button is gone), and it takes the game name from `getPickerCopy(locale)` so the picker and the in-game chrome cannot disagree.
- Star Battle **every colour is now one contiguous region**, from the player's 「同一颜色的格子组成的色块全部连续，不分裂」. Measured before changing anything: the chain construction produced **zero** fully-connected boards at *every* tier, and `starter` was the worst, not the hard tier — the worst region split into 13 pieces at n = 10 and 25 at n = 15, because a region was a horizontal strip with a hole punched by the neighbouring region's star. Research established that connectivity is reachable *inside* the construction's existing validity-window freedom, so uniqueness and the propagation certificate are untouched. Shipped as **strips + a sea** (`construct.ts`): the first two stars take the two edge columns, each tier lays a set of horizontal strips, and everything unclaimed absorbs into one sea region. Connectivity is structural — the generator counts components per colour and rejects any board that splits. Measured 100% across {4,5,6,8,10,12,13,15} × 3 tiers × 4 seeds, and independently re-verified with a union-find traversal written separately from the implementing lane's flood fill. Wave bands keep their tier order and no existing floor moved. The n = 4/5 path is not the briefed uniform-random assignment — that measured 7/2000 and 0/2000 connected, so it was replaced by a parented-domino construction, exhaustively validated at n = 4 (only two admissible permutations exist there) and sampled 2000× at n = 5.
- A derived Star Battle **palette** optimised for maximum minimum pairwise perceptual distance, from the player's 「颜色较多的时候不太容易区分相邻颜色」. The old palette's worst pair measured ΔE00 ≈ 4 — effectively indistinguishable adjacent cells, which is the complaint itself. The new rule is a 5 hue slots × 4 lightness levels grid, the hue shifting a further 36° per level so cross-level pairs differ in hue as well as lightness, optimised over every prefix of 4..20 colours and validated against Sharma CIEDE2000 reference values. Worst pair is now 18.4 light / 14.7 dark at n = 15, and 18.0 / 13.7 at n = 20.
